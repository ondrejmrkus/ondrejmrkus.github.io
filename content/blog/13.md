## How to build new habits

The first week of starting a habit is the hardest for me because it's not settled in my routine yet. Once it becomes automatic, it's easy to keep doing it.

The key to success in building habits is knowing exactly WHAT is supposed to happen, WHEN, and WHERE. When I decide all this in advance, it's more likely it will happen.

> I will \[BEHAVIOR\] at \[TIME\] in \[LOCATION\].

In [Atomic Habits](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/40121378-atomic-habits?from_search=true&from_srp=true&qid=xWAet5MRFu&rank=1), James Clear calls this the **implementation intention**. And the easiest way to use it in your life is to piggyback new habits on those you already have.

> After \[CURRENT HABIT\] I will \[NEW HABIT\].

For example, I already do the same sequence of actions every morning. It goes something like this:

1.  Wake up
2.  Bathroom
3.  Make coffee/tea
4.  **Meditate <<**
5.  Write [Morning Pages](https://www.ondrejmarkus.com/mp/)
6.  Start working

I used this and inserted meditation between making tea and journaling. This way, I know exactly when it's supposed to happen and it does. Once I walk into my studio with tea in hand, I sit down and meditate. No decisions are needed. Works like a charm.

![Meditation](https://www.ondrejmarkus.com/content/images/2021/10/meditationg.png)

"Meditating"

Also, I make new habits easier to do than I could. That's why I meditate only for 5 minutes. The first few weeks are mainly about making the habit stick to my life, not the performance itself. I will do 10 and then 20 minutes once the habit sticks.

## How to test your ideas when everyone is lying to you

I co-run an [entrepreneurship seminar](https://www.inovacnilaborator.cz/) (CZ) at the Charles University in Prague, and we had our first workshop of this semester last Wednesday.

Our students are [first-time entrepreneurs](https://www.ondrejmarkus.com/build/) working on their own ideas for business. We work with them in a combination of workshops and [mastermind groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mastermind_group).

In workshops, we show them the basic tools of [how to design new products](https://www.ondrejmarkus.com/3-levels/) and services. And mastermind sessions are about [showing their ideas](https://www.ondrejmarkus.com/tweet-prototype/) to people, helping each other, and making progress faster.

The main topic of this week was how to talk to potential customers to figure out if their idea is good.

![](https://www.ondrejmarkus.com/content/images/2021/10/ask-questionss-2.png)

Asking good questions is hard.

To answer this for them, I mostly borrowed from [The Mom Test](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/52283963-the-mom-test?ac=1&from_search=true&qid=Nn3P3i66kt&rank=1) – a great little book about customer interviews.

![The Mom Test](https://www.ondrejmarkus.com/content/images/2021/10/1200x630wz.png)

The Mom Test

In summary, the rules for doing good customer interviews are this:

1.  Talk about their life instead of your idea
2.  Ask about specifics in the past instead of generics or opinions about the future
3.  Talk less and listen more

If you're trying to get your ideas off the ground, I fully recommend reading this book. It's very good and short. My favorite combo.
